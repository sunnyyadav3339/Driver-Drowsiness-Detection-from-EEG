\section{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}

Table~\ref{tab:datasets} lists the public datasets used by the reviewed studies. Three features matter for explanation. First, the evidence base is narrow. Twelve of the 15 EEG papers use the Cao \textit{et al.} lane-departure dataset \cite{cao2019multichannel} or SEED-VIG \cite{zheng2017multimodal}. Eight use Cao, and seven of those use the same 11-subject subset, so many claims about what EEG models learn rest on one simulator, one labelling rule and about eleven drivers.

Second, datasets differ in what their labels mean: reaction time, PERCLOS, self-report, observer rating, eye state or the recording session. An explanation can only be as meaningful as the label it explains. MRL Eye and CEW, for instance, record whether the eye is open and do not measure drowsiness at all.

Third, a dataset limits what an explanation can claim. SEED-VIG has temporal and posterior channels only, so it cannot directly support statements about prefrontal activity \cite{xu2026dynamic}, and any band finding at its temporal sites has to contend with muscle activity \cite{gao2024sftnet}. Studies also use differing subsets of SEED-VIG, including 14, 21 and 23 subjects \cite{gao2025comprehensive,zhou2026sedriven,gao2024sftnet}, which complicates comparison between them.

Many studies use private data \cite{seo2025driver,jiang2026eeg,wu2024driver,gao2025comprehensive,hasan2024validation,puspasari2026predicting,nomura2025drowsiness,zhou2021predicting,zhu2025madfm,guan2025fatigue,zeynalpour2025multimethod}, which prevents independent checks of their explanations. Others name no dataset \cite{jha2025realtime,sasikala2026robust,nayomi2026vision} or one we could not trace \cite{theivadas2025dynamic}.
